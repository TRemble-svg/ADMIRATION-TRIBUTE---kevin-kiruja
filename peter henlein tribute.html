01 · The man behind the mechanism

A craftsman ahead of his time

Peter Henlein was a German locksmith and clockmaker from Nuremberg born in 1485, He is credited for the invention of the pocket watch. He was among the first craftsmen to make a small portable spring driven watch,he also made a classic fire gilded pomanda shaped watch. In his own way he curved a path for the modern watch market to make beautiful artpieces we love in our time.

He brought forth the creation of the pocketwacth an invention tha massively helped the ship and long distance traders workers of the time, he brought fashion with the pendant watch, making a time piece a symbol of fashion and style which we appreciate to this day.

His story is one of patience,precision and engineering that is to be celebrated and admired, peter henlein is a man who understood that machines dont have to be dull or boring to perfom. This is a man who worked with small intricate parts in watchmaking and saw the beauty in time itself, what makes his story more interesting was the place where he learned clock making, he requested for asylum after being involved in a brawl where a man lost his life and he spent His days there making a device like no other.

He was known as the first craftsman to build clockworks into "Bisamköpfe", musk-balls or pomanders, small pendant containers fashioned from precious metals for fragrances or disinfectants; these are now known as pomander watches, In November 1509, he became a master in the city's locksmith guild. He is mentioned in the city's records as the supplier of these small clocks, which were given as gifts to important people.The earliest extant example of a watch, the Watch 1505, a fire-gilded pomander watch dated 1505, has been attributed to Henlein.

In 1529, Henlein traveled to Strasbourg on behalf of the Nuremberg council, for a sky globe. Six years later, he crafted a watch for the council of Nuremberg.He also built a tower clock for Lichtenau castle in 1541, and was known as a builder of advanced astronomical instruments.

Henlein died in August 1542 and was buried at the Katharinenkirche, Nuremberg

02 · The art of keeping time

Small clocks, big possibilities

Early portable clocks used a coiled metal mainspring instead of the heavy hanging weights found in many larger clocks. This made it possible to build smaller mechanisms that could be carried or worn. Henlein was among the early makers associated with this important development.

A contemporary account from the early 1510s praised a Nuremberg maker named Peter Hele—generally identified with Peter Henlein—for clocks that could be carried at the chest or in a purse and run for around forty hours.

The watchmaker's achievement

His importance lies in the craft of miniaturising mechanical clockwork: making intricate gears and spring-powered mechanisms work inside a much smaller object.

Historical note: Henlein is often called the inventor of the pocket watch, but portable timepieces existed before his career. Historians therefore describe his role with care.

03 · A life in time

His story and lasting influence

  • c. 1485

    Peter Henlein is believed to have been born in Nuremberg, in what is now Germany.

  • 1509

    He became a master locksmith in Nuremberg, confirming his standing as a skilled craftsperson.

  • 1511–12

    A published account describes small, spring-driven clocks made by Peter Hele that could be carried on the body.

  • 1542

    Henlein died in Nuremberg, leaving behind a reputation connected to the early history of portable clocks.

Why I admire him

I admire Peter Henlein because his work represents curiosity, patience and dedication to a craft. He worked in a time when creating a tiny, reliable mechanism demanded extraordinary skill. His example reminds us that progress often begins with someone willing to improve the details.Borrowing on the existing and improving to create perfection.

A legacy that continues

Today, watches are part of everyday life, from traditional mechanical pieces to modern smartwatches. The history of portable timekeeping is much larger than one person, but Henlein remains an important figure in its early story.
